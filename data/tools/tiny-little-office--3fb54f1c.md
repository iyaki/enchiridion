# The **Link Is This Document**

If you look at the link for this document, it starts with: `https://tinylittleoffice.com/#` Followed by lots of letters. Those letters are a compressed version of this document.

On the web, things that come after “#” are never sent to the server. They only live in your browser.

---

So the way this worked is:

1. You clicked on this link. That **loaded the document data** into your browser—but it didn’t know what to do with it.
2. Your browser asked for the website at TinyLittleOffice. **That’s the code.**
3. The **code ran** in your browser, and it **converted and displayed** the data after the “#” and painted it onto your screen.
4. Now you see the document!

---

See that giant QR code? That is this document. It is also a link to the document. And you can insert it into the document.

# This is now your copy of the document. You can’t change the old link. But you can edit it and share the new link.

**It can be very, very private.** If you make documents in a web browser in incognito mode, then share them with only your friends on private chat like Signal, then no company or AI bot will be able to read your stuff.

**But if you want everyone to read your words,** just share the link, or the QR code (even on a sticker), and no one can erase it, because it’s not saved anywhere.

# So it’s either totally ephemeral and private, or immutable and public, and that’s up to you.
